\section{Quick-Reference Guides}\label{app:quickref}

\subsection*{B.1 Faculty: one-page guide}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=0.5cm]
  \item[Go ahead] Use AI for planning, content, rubrics, feedback drafts, admin work, and research support. No approval needed for non-personal data.
  \item[Declare] Put an AI-use level (0--4) in your syllabus; Level~2 applies if you say nothing.
  \item[Design] Match assessments to outcomes; stage work; add short oral or in-class checks for high stakes; test your tasks with AI.
  \item[Protect data] De-identify student data; use Tier~B tools for personal data; never paste confidential records into unlisted tools.
  \item[Own the result] Review AI output; you remain accountable for grades and content.
  \item[Handle concerns] Talk to the student first; use process evidence; never rely on an AI detector alone.
  \item[Get help] Contact the Support Desk; join the practice community; share what works.
\end{description}

\subsection*{B.2 Students: one-page guide}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=0.5cm]
  \item[Read] Check each course's AI-use level and each assignment's notes; ask if unclear.
  \item[Use to learn] Use AI to understand, practice, and improve, not to skip the learning.
  \item[Check] Verify facts and sources; you are responsible for what you submit.
  \item[Disclose] Attach a short AI Use Declaration to every submission (``No AI used'' if none); keep drafts and notes.
  \item[Protect] Do not upload others' personal data or confidential materials; mind your own privacy.
  \item[If accused] You will be heard: you can explain your process, see the evidence, and appeal.
  \item[Need access?] Tell your instructor if you cannot access a required tool.
\end{description}
